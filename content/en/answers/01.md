1. Vertical scaling limited to a pair of controllers, dependence on one vendor's hardware and software, and data islands (a separate product for block, file and object). Ceph removes them by scaling horizontally on ordinary servers and serving all three interfaces on one shared RADOS.
2. A RAID rebuild is limited by the write speed of one spare disk, so its duration grows linearly as disks get bigger. In Ceph the data of a failed OSD is spread over hundreds of PGs, and all healthy OSDs read and write in parallel; the more OSDs there are, the smaller each one's share and the faster the repair.
3. The MON only gives the cluster map to the client and takes part in the consensus on map changes. Data does not pass through the MON; the client writes directly to the primary OSD, and the primary OSD coordinates replication to the secondaries.
4. The PG aggregates billions of objects into a few thousand groups, so that peering, recovery and scrub happen at the group level. Without it, OSDs would have to track the state of each object separately, and the memory and messaging overhead would become unbearable.
5. A majority (quorum) is needed to make decisions; an odd number gives the highest failure tolerance for the fewest members. Four MONs need a majority of three and tolerate only one failure, the same as three MONs.
6. `size` is the desired number of copies, and `min_size` is the minimum number of available copies needed to accept I/O. With `min_size=1` a write may be acknowledged on only one OSD, and if that OSD fails, acknowledged data is lost.
7. Separation is useful when replication and recovery traffic is heavy enough to compete with client traffic. The risk is that if the cluster network partly fails, OSDs report each other down while the MON sees them as alive, and flapping follows.
8. Ceph flushes on every durable write; an SSD without PLP must really write its cache to flash and becomes very slow, and on a power loss it may lose acknowledged data.
9. A minimal `ceph.conf`, `ceph.client.admin.keyring` and `ceph.pub`. The most sensitive file is the admin keyring, which gives full access to the cluster.
10. The increase of `mon_target_pg_per_osd` from 100 to 200 and of `mon_max_pg_per_osd` from 250 to 500, which can make pools grow their PG count after the upgrade. (The change to the mClock scheduler's shard settings for HDD is another example.)
