1. mgr, then the mons one by one, then the OSDs and finally the other services; the mgr is first because the cephadm module that drives the upgrade runs in the mgr and must be the new version.
2. All OSDs of one failure domain are upgraded together after ok-to-upgrade confirms it; the upgrade time of a large cluster drops sharply without breaking availability.
3. The OSD stays in the destroyed state with the same ID and CRUSH position; so only that OSD's PGs are refilled and no cluster-wide redistribution happens.
4. With osd set-group it sets noout on the host's CRUSH subtree, and removes it on exit.
5. pg_target = the pool's capacity ratio × (number of OSDs in the root × mon_target_pg_per_osd) × bias / size, then rounding to a power of 2.
6. It prevents small, frequent pg_num changes (a change only on a threefold difference); the dynamic threshold applies to increases to 512 and 1024 (threshold 1) and 2048 (threshold 2).
7. The value went from 100 to 200; after the upgrade the autoscaler may split PGs and data moves.
8. It finds OSDs whose deviation from their weighted share exceeds max_deviation and moves a PG from a full OSD to an underloaded one, provided the CRUSH rule is not violated.
9. It is applied on top of the CRUSH output and is ignored and removed if it violates the rule; a weight change alters the whole mapping and is not predictable.
10. ceph config set persists in the MON database and is reversible; injectargs only temporarily changes the value in the daemon's memory.
