1. max, pool, the bitwise key (the reversed hash), nspace, the effective key (if either of the two keys is non-empty), oid and finally snap.
2. So that the low bits of the hash, which determine the PG, move to the top of the key and all objects of a PG form one contiguous range in the sort order.
3. Into two contiguous halves: PG 0x1f gets the range [0xf8000000, 0xfbffffff] and the new PG 0x3f gets [0xfc000000, 0xffffffff].
4. `_` is the object_info_t (version, size, flags, digests, watchers) and `snapset` is the SnapSet of the head object.
5. shard = ps % num_shards; on SSD the default is 8 shards and 2 threads per shard.
6. waiting_for_unreadable_object, with the text "waiting for missing object".
7. By looking up the reqid in caller_ops, extra_caller_ops and dup_index; dups keep the result of requests whose log entry has been trimmed.
8. max(the PG's last_user_version, the object's user_version) + 1 (and at least at_version.version); because the counter increases at the PG level, not at the object level.
9. When all members of acting (including the primary itself) have reported the commit.
10. %010u.%020llu from epoch and version (with the prefix dup_ for a dup); the fixed length makes string order the same as numeric order.
