1. Several ready transactions are recorded one after another in one WriteBatch, and only the last of them is sync. One fsync on the WAL makes them all durable.
2. 7 bytes: masked crc32c (4), length (2) and type (1). In the recyclable format a 4-byte log number is added as well.
3. Because short-lived keys (deferred and pg log) are deleted while memtables are merged and never reach an SST, which greatly reduces write amplification.
4. Because deferred (`L`) and pgmeta (`P`) keys live very briefly, and more merging means more deletions before the flush.
5. Without reading a data block, it determines that the key is probably not in this file, and it removes most of the useless reads from L0 to Ln.
6. About 64G (1G × 8 × 8).
7. rocksdb_original puts only whole levels on the DB. use_some_extra uses the extra space for part of the next level.
8. Bytes 0 to 12 of the key are hashed with ceph_str_hash_rjenkins, and the remainder modulo 3 gives the CF number.
9. So that walking the omap of an object or PG happens in one CF with one range scan.
10. Because important defaults (such as memtable merging and LZ4) are removed unintentionally. `bluestore_rocksdb_options_annex` adds only the changed keys.
